# Game design

The hole moves on XZ, with Y representing height. Hold the primary mouse button or a touch on the city to steer toward that location; release stops movement. Targets refresh against the following orthographic camera. Speed is a fixed 3.2 units per second. The cursor is visible. View size is 6.2 + radius × 2.6; a ResizeObserver keeps the camera and rendering canvas synchronized during phone-preview and orientation changes.

The map is 200×200 world units with a 20-unit street grid: 100 compact mixed-use city blocks, each with pocket parks. Buildings occupy interior lots. Trees are park-only. Street furniture and people belong on sidewalks. Moving cars and vans follow right-hand lanes, respect alternating signals, stop before crossings, keep safe queue spacing, and clear intersections before the crossing direction turns green. Parked cars sit along the curb. Pedestrians reflect at sidewalk endpoints, avoid overlapping their neighbors, and animate with shoulder/hip joint pivots. Seeded variation controls colors and orientation; each size tier cycles through four distinct designs so all four occur in the city. Facade frames and dark windows stand out against colored walls, and roofs vary between gabled, flat, stepped, and spired silhouettes.

Each of 14 ascending footprint sizes awards one term in the Fibonacci sequence beginning 1, 2, 3, 5. All completed objects contribute to global score, so previously edible objects always help progression. Levels use score thresholds and fixed radii, with no separate category gates or quotas. Beyond the skyscraper level, each new level adds 0.7 radius and costs ceil(10000 × (1 + log2(n + 2))) additional points, starting with extra index n=0. Growth remains uncapped.

Capture transitions idle → falling. Objects are pulled toward the moving hole at full scale, then descend with quadratic acceleration. Ground-plane clipping keeps upper geometry visible until it passes through the aperture. Tall objects have longer fall durations. Objects transition falling → swallowed and award their points/mass exactly once after their complete fall. Remaining count includes falling objects; victory waits for zero remaining.

The road/park map is painted once into a canvas texture and displayed as a single plane, eliminating overlapping thin ground geometry. WebGL handles GPU rendering and material clipping. The non-WebGL raster fallback projects Three.js triangles into one canvas and clips falling polygons below ground, avoiding SVG DOM overhead. Visible objects are culled against the camera frustum before rendering; HUD updates are throttled.

The HUD is a single compact left panel with an expandable map. Camera zoom smoothly follows radius. In the raster fallback, object-level painter ordering and explicit facade detail layers keep window planes from being overwritten by their backing wall triangles.
