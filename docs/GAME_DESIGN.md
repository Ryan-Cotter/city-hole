# Game design and implementation

The authoritative city is a seeded array of lightweight records, with an ID-to-slot map. Three.js models are loaded only inside visible chunks and an object frustum test. Completed records are swap-deleted and removed from chunk, movement, support and model lists. Shared geometry and materials remain reusable; unique merged-block geometry is disposed when replaced or unloaded.

The 120-meter square uses 20-meter blocks. Residential blocks favor houses, commercial blocks shops and offices, industrial blocks warehouses, downtown blocks taller buildings and skyscrapers, and park blocks small facilities. Pocket parks occupy southern corners. Cars follow straight street lanes, stop at red/yellow signals, keep queue spacing and wrap at the outer road edge. Pedestrians reflect at block ends and separate along shared lanes. Nearby/visible motion is simulated regardless of object size tier. Returning off-screen actors resume from their persistent last position; they are not despawned or teleported.

A radius-0.04 hole starts in a tiny section with bottlecaps, dice, stones, coins, toys, food and mice. Areas have half-widths 3, 10 and 60. Swallowing a glowing yard-border segment unlocks the block-sized area; swallowing its glowing boundary opens the city. Unlocks happen only on completed swallowing. The minimap fits the unlocked section and reduces detail at larger radii. The camera uses `0.7 + 1.3 × radius` resting view size and smooths toward a 25% pullback while moving.

Ascending object footprints receive Fibonacci rewards beginning 1, 2. The initial growth curve uses `max(ceil(16 × log2(level)), 3 × previous-tier reward)` increments and small radius margins at tiny scales. Beyond countries, incremental requirements remain logarithmic. Geographic phases reset local growth thresholds without resetting the accumulated score.

Campaign flow: starter yard → block-sized area → city → building-only expansion at 80% cleared → landscape → countries → Next city/era. Whole-block records reference their contained static objects, which complete exactly once when the block finishes falling. Motion actors retain their own records. Yellow lightning pickups give 10 seconds of speed; green triple arrows increase one level preserving fractional progress.

Normal objects transition idle → falling → swallowed. The fall starts at the object's actual height, pulls toward the moving hole, and accelerates below the ground while ground clipping hides submerged geometry. Only completion awards rewards. A supported food item has its own record and score; when its table falls, unsupported food descends to the ground unless independently captured.

Objects five or more tiers below current capacity keep their original visible shape without animation. They complete immediately once center distance plus footprint radius is within the hole. This deliberately skips their falling animation. Every reward still uses the same exact-once completion path. At radius 8 or greater, distant blocks beyond the interaction area render as one merged building mesh. Nearby blocks retain individual falling objects; aggregate rendering does not create aggregate scoring.

Movement allows 30% radius overhang at map/section edges, enough to reach corner objects. Circle and ring vertices use a continuous exponential compression curve near boundaries. The Canvas renderer uses the same sampled outline. Footprint capture remains circular rather than recomputing deforming polygon physics.

Self play retains valid routes, prefers direct clear paths, and uses bounded A* with no diagonal corner cuts otherwise. Its goals honor section unlocks and allow edge targets through clamped destinations. Obsolete tiny rewards are lower priority until relevant targets are exhausted. Self play uses the same radius-scaled speed, solid-object collisions, captures and scoring; it does not teleport or inject points.

WebGL uses procedural grain, flat-shaded materials and lights. The Canvas fallback caches triangles, shades faces and adds subtle contact shadows; it remains a compatibility path with slower large-view performance. Electron provides a local sandboxed native window with GPU acceleration enabled by default. The verification environment cannot provide a hardware GL context, so native performance targets require real desktop/mobile testing.

Growth reserves: `foodBudget.ts` checks each new size/section for edible unlocked points totaling 1.5 times the full next-level cost. It fills deficits using phase-appropriate items and stops at the largest required footprint for that map. Bug movement uses phase-offset two-axis scurrying, bounded speed, and nearby-hole acceleration. Self-play obstacles use the same circular footprints as collision resolution and scale the search grid with hole size.
